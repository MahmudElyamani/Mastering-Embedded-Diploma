-Clocks are the beating heart of every digital circuit found inside any IC

-Digital circuits must all sync with one another using clocks so that operations are step by step

-Any microcontroller must have a clock in order to function, that's why clocks are considered the heart of the IC

-Clocks are used by the core and by the periphrals

-When the frequency of a clock increases, its power consumbtion is also increased. That's why there are power management unit modules 
 on some SOC's specifically designed to turn off the clock or decrease its frequency during stand-by or low power mode.
-A periphral with a slow clock uses the least amount of power.

-In most MCU's there's a clock management unit that governs the clock of every periphral so that the power consumbtion is managed.

-The clock_source is the part responsible for generating the clock, it could be located within the MCU or outside of it depending
 on the MCU, this is also known as a "Crystal Oscillator".

What to look for in a datasheet when opening the clock segment?:
• first check the MCU module diagram for the word clock and see what its called, in STM32 manual you'll see it's RCC
• Next from the table of context open the RCC page and locate your exact MCU, e.g STM32F103X
• Identify the types of clock sources it has, e.g HSI oscillator clock, HSE oscillator clock, PLL clock.
• Open the RCC registers and look at its different pirephrals, e.g clock control register (RCC_CR)
• in STM32 the PLL clock is what runs the system clock and cannot be turned off, but if we want to turn it off we can oder the 
  HSI timer to run the system clock then turn off the PLL.
• The RCC clock in the STM is connected to the AHB bus according to the MCU diagram, so to find its base address we must open the
  memory map and locate RCC and we'll find its address range.
• we can find the registers of RCC next to it in sections and it'll lay out a map of every RCC pirephral.

-Some MCU have both internal and external clocks, in order to pick which one to operate from you have to manipulate the register.

What's the clock tree diagram?:
• a diagram that shows the connections of internal and external clocks as well as the speeds of each.
• this lets you understand each protocol's speed and how does it get its speed.
• for example in the STM32 Trm the external oscilator is connected to HSE which accepts a 4-16MHZ crystal, that signal is
  given to the user as an option and is alos divided by 2 and passed through PLL which allows you to multiply it by x3, x4,...,x16
  to access different frequencies.
• you'll also find in the diagram that the maximum frequency you can output is 72MHz so that's something to keep in mind when using
  the PLL multiplier and choosing the external crystal frequency.
• There's also an internal clock that resonates at 8MHZ called HSI connected to the bus as an option.
• this distinction is important because this allows you to choose which frequency you like based on the firmware you write.
* You can view the clock diagram in a cleaner and more interactive way within the "STM32CubeIDE" that lets you manipulate time.

if you take a look back at the MCU diagram you'll find that the AHB bus operates at 72MHZ, but the APB1 bus is at 24/36 MHZ, why?:
• According to the clock tree, the 72 MHZ clock frequency went through a pre-scaler through AHB that divides the frequency.
• each clock and timer is shown in the clock diagram that shows why each clock is that quick.
• these pre-scalers exist to optimize power consumbtion, as not all pirephrals need high speeds to operate.
