-Unlike SPI, I2C is a protocol that can operate on multiple masters, although some MCU allow SPI to operate using multiple masters 
like the STM32, this is usually not standardized and is done in specific MCU as a side feature.

-I2C uses 2 bi-directional open-drain pins for data communication.
-Open-drain mode defaults between two states, either open and the pin becomes floating or drain and the pin becomes logic 0.
-A very important implementation of the open-drain topology is the logic AND gate operation, meaning that the data line pulled
up by a logic high resistor connected to two or more open-drain pins will always remain LOW unless all open-drain pins are set
to high impedance mode (logic 1)

• By default all devices connected to the I2C data lines are high impedance mode, meaning they're all detecting logic High at the moment
• Once a master wants to issue orders it goes to drain mode sinking current from main supply and changing the bus line logic.
• When that happens all other devices on the line acknoledge the master's action and start taking input.
• If multiple masters issued the same drain command at once to start giving instructions, the master who issued the command first
  even by a split second is going to get priority, the other master will wait until the data line is no longer pulled low to issue
  its command.

Explain the I2C Bus characteristics:
• The framing for I2C consists of a start bit, then the slave address following the command and the data to be sent then stop bit.
• Each slave in the data network should have its own address in order to distinguish between devices.
• the command bit is for issuing whether to read or to write from the slave.
• Each byte in the data bits is followed by an acknoledgement from the slave to tell the master the byte's been received.

** Unlike SPI, I2C has an acknowledge mechanism which ensures the signal goes to its right place (Interview_question)

How does starting or stopping transmission works?:
• The start and stop conditions are generated by the master.
• The start and stop conditions are initiated by keeping the level of SCL line as HIGH then changing the level of SDA line.
• The start condition is initialized when SDA goes from HIGH to LOW while SCL line is at HIGH.
• The stop condition is initialized when SDA goes from LOW to HIGH while SCL line is at HIGH.

** This is why data is only transmitted when the SCL line goes to LOW, that way the MCU doesn't read a state switch on the SDA
line as a STOP or START operation. (Interview_Question)

-Since data transmission happens when the SCL line goes to LOW then data reading or (sampling) happens when the SCL line goes HIGH
this means the MCU is not allowed to transmit any other bit during the reading data operation.

How does a slave return an acknowledge after receiving data from the master?: (Interview_Question)
• There's a sequence of operations that happens during the ACK part of the data processing:
• First the master pulls the SCL line to low
• then slave pulls SDA line to low
• master then issues a clock pulse on SCL line by pulling the SCL line to HIGH for a pulse.
• If the master sees SDA at a logic LOW state during the SCL pulse that means the slave acknowledges the byte and SDA is pulled HIGH
• If the master sees SDA at a logic HIGH state during the SCL pulse that means the slave failed to pull SDA line to LOW thereby failing
  to acknowledge the data sent by the master correctly.

How does a master return an acknowledge after receiving data from the slave?:
• The slave sends the last data bit then immediately releases the SDA line which means it will go to HIGH.
• Master will then pull the SDA to LOW.
• Master will then issue a clock pulse through the SCL line
• Master will then release the SDA line and slave will regain control of it.

What is I2C Arbitration?:
• If 2 masters issues a transmit command at the same time an arbitration process begins.
• the masters send the first bit as they normally would by pulling SDA line low or keeping it high.
• They then would read the voltage level on the line to see if it is what the device expects.
• If the device sees that the line has been driven low when it is expected to be idle HIGH that means another master is in control
• It will then lose arbitration and wait for a stop ondition before attempting to transmit its message again.

-There's a concept called clock stretching which is when the clock sent by the master is too fast for the slave to store at the same time.
In this case the slave pulls the SCL line to LOW until it stores all its data, then the master sees this and stops sending data through SDA
until the slave lets go of it.
once the SCL line is no longer pulled low the master then takes control of the SCL line and sends a clock pulse to get an ACK singal.
Not all devices have this clock stretching features, especially slave devices. However every master should support this feature.
