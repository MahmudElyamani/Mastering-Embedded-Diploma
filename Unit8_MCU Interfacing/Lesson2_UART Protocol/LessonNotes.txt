-Data Skew is a phenomenon that occurs when multiple bits are sent simultaneously through a parallel interface, some bits arrive
later than others due to propagation delay, this causes issues with signal integrity.

-When there's a shared clock between transmitter and receiver, the transmitter sends a signal at the falling or rising edge of the
clock pulse, the receiver then takes a sample on the rising or falling edge respectively on that same clock pulse.

-There's a clear distinction between the clock frequency the MCU operates at and the clock frequency it uses to communicate with
other MCU or sensor.
If you get asked at an interview, there's 2 MCU's one of them operates at 800MHZ and the other at 1MHZ how can they communicate?
• It's most likely a trick question trying to confuse you between CPU bit rate and communication baud rate.
• An MCU that operates at 1MHZ means that it can handle up to 1 million instructions per second.
• The MCU has separate clock peripherals that allow you to adjust the clock speed by prescaling it to reach the desired communication speed.
• An MCU that communicates at 9600 baud rate means that it sends up to 9600 symbols per second to another MCU.
• A symbol usually consists of multiple bits at a time but in the world of embedded system a symbol is only 1 bit.
• Identifying the baud rate lets the MCU know the single clock's pulse width and thus when exactly to take samples for receiving.
• It does so by a simple equation: 9600 baud rate = (1/9600) = 104.167 uS.


-Synchronous Communication Protocols: SPI - I2C - USART
-Asynchronous Communication Protocols: UART - CAN

-Dynamic Data Rate: A baud rate that can be changed during runtime, example: Synchronous Communication Protocols.
-Static Data Rate: A baud rate that cannot be changed during runtime, example: Asynchronous Communication Protocols.

-An Asynchronous data transmission requires a stop bit and start bit at the very least while a Synchronous one does not necessarily
require one due to the shared clock line but can sill have one for added security/assurance.

-Throughput: is the amount of effective data sent to the amount of actual data sent.
For example, if you transmit data at 800 Bits per second, but to transmit 8 bits of data you need 1 start bit and 1 end bit
so you need 10 bits total to transmit 8 bits, this means the actual effictive data your transmitting is actually (8/10)*800
= 640 Bits per second, this number is known as throughput.

-UART is the most used communication protocol in the world of embedded Linux and embedded systems.

-The @Framing of the data is the structure of the bits sent and their function, for examble some framing could have a pairty
check bit and some do not, some framing could have 2 stop bits and some only 1.

Regular Framing for a data structure:
=========================================-------------============------------
| @Start |         @Data_Bits           |   @parity   |   @Stop  |   @Stop   |
|   1    |        5    ->    9 bits     |   0 -> 1    |     1    |  0 -> 1   |
|        |                              |   Optional  |          |  Optional |
=========================================-------------============------------

-When sending the bits it is important to distinguish between order of sending, whether you're sending from LSB or MSB.
For most UART protocols the data bits are sent starting from the LSB unless it specifically states that it's big endian, in
which case the bits are order from the MSB order.

-CTS and RTS are additional pins to the USART protocol used in flow control applications.
